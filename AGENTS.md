# Puzzlet: working rules

A calm jigsaw puzzle game for ages 3 to 5. One child, one picture, pieces
that click home. Native Android, paid once, fully offline: no ads, no
trackers, no accounts, no network. Open source under MIT.

## This file is not the final word

These rules are the current best understanding, not the ceiling. If a change
you believe in contradicts one, do not silently drop the idea and do not
implement against the rule either: name the conflict, make the case, and let
the owner decide. An overridden rule is updated here, never left as a dead
letter.

Every session starts with two acts, in this order: read this file, then
`git fetch` and pull whatever is new on `main`. The owner works from more
than one machine; never build on a stale head.

## Hard constraints (non-negotiable)

1. **No music.** When sound arrives (M2), effects are synthesized,
   deterministic, and inharmonic, regenerated by `:tools` from code; a single
   pitched chime at most, never twice inside 1200 ms, because two pitched
   notes in sequence make an interval and intervals are where melody starts.
2. **No depiction of animate beings.** No humans, animals, faces, mascots,
   characters, or eyes-on-objects, in the app, the launcher icon, or store
   art. Only inanimate subjects: vehicles, houses, boats, planes, trees,
   flowers, fruit, sky, sun, moon, stars, geometric patterns.
3. **Zero manifest permissions.** This underpins the Data-safety declaration
   and the Families listing. No `INTERNET`, no exceptions. Do not add one
   without the owner signing off in this file first.
4. **English only.** No localization infrastructure, no translated resources,
   until the owner asks. Strings live in `res/values/strings.xml`, nowhere
   else.
5. **Nothing may feel like AI slop.** No placeholder copy, no dead buttons,
   no lorem text, no generic purple-gradient Material defaults, no stock
   iconography, no filler screens. Every string is a real sentence, every
   color is chosen, every asset is drawn for this app. A missing piece is
   listed below as a known gap, never faked.
6. **The app must not crash.** Defensive code, no `!!`, no unchecked casts,
   state that survives rotation and backgrounding, and tests around every
   rule. A Play-vitals crash is a stop-the-line event.

## Style

**No em-dashes, ever, unless absolutely necessary.** Not in chat replies,

colons, parentheses, or a sentence break instead.

**Plain-text store text.** Release notes are pasted into Play Console, where

## Shipping constraints

- `applicationId` is permanently `app.puzzlet`. Play ties an app to its
  first package ID forever. The code namespace mirrors it.
- `versionCode` only ever increases and is never reused. `versionName` is
  `versionCode` divided by ten, one decimal: 1 means 0.1, 2 means 0.2, 9
  means 0.9, 10 means 1.0, 11 means 1.1, and so on forever. The owner set
  the walk explicitly: 0.1, 0.2, and so on. The first store appearance
  (closed testing) is versionCode 1, versionName 0.1; Play never saw any
  earlier build, so the walk starts honestly at its first step.
- `targetSdk` moves only together with an AGP that supports it.
- The merged manifest carries one app-private, signature-scoped permission
  (`app.puzzlet.DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION`), added by
  androidx.core for dynamically registered receivers, exactly as in the
  house's shipped apps. The zero-permissions rule means: we declare
  nothing dangerous, nothing normal, nothing that touches the network.
  Do not remove core-ktx to chase this line away.
- The signing keystore lives outside the repo and will live in the
  `KEYSTORE_BASE64` GitHub secret once created. If it is lost the app can
  never be updated again. M0 has no keystore; CI builds unsigned.
- Paid once on Play Console (owner handles the merchant profile and the
  upload). No billing SDK in the app, ever.

## The game

Scatter, drag, snap, celebrate. It is all live in M1: three pictures
(sailboat, rocket, house), the ladder of 4, 6, 9, 12, 16, 20 and 24 pieces
chosen with picture buttons, drag-and-snap with a generous tolerance, the
faint ghost board always visible, a peek coin that strengthens it, a soft
honey ring on the slot while a piece is held, and a ring-burst plus haptic
tick when a piece clicks home. No timer, no score, no fail state, no reading
required. Backing out mid-game keeps the progress for the session, and the
session itself survives a process death: at most one unfinished picture is
kept on disk and re-seated on return.

Sound is live, the house way: four synthesized, deterministic, inharmonic
effects (pick, drop, snap, and one pitched chime at completion only, never
twice inside 1200 ms), generated by :tools, pinned by checkSounds, gated by
the sound switch on the shelf. Real store screenshots render from the app's
own states in CI (three form factors, API 35).

Known gaps (honest, not faked): the Play Console listing and first upload
(owner-side; everything they need is in play-store/), and the polish passes
that only play can reveal.

## Architecture

```
core/     pure Kotlin, zero Android imports: cut, scenes, board rules
host/     ViewModel: which screen, which piece in hand, resumes, sounds
ui/       Compose: gallery, difficulty chooser, play field, celebration
theme     PuzzletColors + Baloo 2 typography; icons drawn as geometry
tools/    offline asset generators: plain JVM Kotlin, Java2D, no libraries
```

Outside the code, two asset homes: play-store/ holds the listing kit
(screenshots in per-form-factor subfolders, feature graphic, store icon,
the submission guide); docs/ holds privacy.html as served on GitHub Pages
and the bundled font's OFL license. Nothing else lives loose.

`:tools:makeIcons` draws the launcher icon set from the design system;
`:tools:checkIcons` fails the build if the committed PNGs drift from a fresh
regeneration. The in-app brand mark reuses the committed foreground PNG, so
the icon has exactly one source of truth. New asset families follow the same
make/check pattern; nothing hand-edited survives without a generator.

The organising principle (inherited from the house): the rules are pure data
and functions; Android is a player of those rules, not a participant. The
play field renders through one Canvas; pieces are the scene clipped by their
own outline, so there is not a single bitmap in gameplay.

## Forbidden

1. No composable without a caller, no button without an action.
2. No user-facing string outside `res/values/strings.xml`.
3. No color literal in UI code; everything comes from `PuzzletColors`.
4. No `!!`, no unchecked casts, no swallowed exceptions.
5. No network code, no WebView, no third-party SDKs. AndroidX and Kotlin
   only; each new dependency is proposed here first.
6. No file over 400 lines, no function over 40. Split early.
7. **Never add a `Co-Authored-By` trailer or any AI attribution to a commit
   message.** GitHub credits trailers, which would put the tool in the
   repository's contributor list. One person writes this app and the history
   should say so.

## Design seeds (chosen, not defaulted)

- Lagoon teal `#0C7A64`: the toy-box lid. Calm, gender-neutral, legible on
  paper in sunlight, distinct from the candy reds of ad-driven kids apps.
- Warm paper `#FAF6EF`: the play surface. Easier on young eyes than pure
  white, flatters the teal, prints well in store screenshots.
- Honey accent arrives with gameplay (stars, confetti); it must not appear
  before there is something to celebrate.
- Display face: Baloo 2 (OFL, bundled, offline), ExtraBold for the name,
  Bold for headings. Body text follows the system font under a 1.3 cap, the
  house rule: past that cap words stop fitting fixed play surfaces.
- Corners are generous (28dp large); nothing sharp touches a child's game.

## Build

```
./gradlew :app:testReleaseUnitTest :app:lintRelease   # unit tests, full lint
./gradlew :tools:checkIcons :tools:checkSounds        # committed assets match their generators
./gradlew :app:assembleRelease                        # R8 release (signed when the keystore is present)
./gradlew :tools:makeIcons :tools:makeSounds :tools:makeArt   # regenerate after a deliberate design change
```

An Android SDK and the Android Studio JBR are installed on the owner's
machine (`JAVA_HOME` must point at the JBR; it is not on PATH). CI is the
loop: `build.yml` gates every push to `main` on tests, lint, generator
determinism (`:tools:test`), the sound pin (`:tools:checkSounds`, safe in
CI because the WAV writer is hand-rolled and byte-stable across JDKs) and
a release build. `:tools:checkIcons` stays a LOCAL pinning gate: run it
after any deliberate icon change and commit the regenerated PNGs in the
same commit (ImageIO PNGs are not byte-stable across JDK versions).

`screenshots.yml` recaptures the store screenshots whenever UI files
change: seven scenes per form factor (phone, 7", 10"), pinned to API 35,
the same image the house uses. Download the three `store-screenshots-*`
artifacts with `gh run download <run-id> -R muntasimulhaque/puzzlet -D <dir>`
and strip the form-factor prefix into `play-store/screenshots/` when the
listing kit is assembled. The celebration scene is mid-animation by design,
so its pixels wobble a little between runs; every other scene should come
back effectively identical. Do not pre-check UI on a local emulator. A push
that touches no listed path runs no CI at all; when a fix commit needs
proof, dispatch manually (`gh workflow run build.yml --ref main`).

## Commits and releasing

Small commits, plain messages, no AI trailers. Bump `versionCode` +1 with
every release-candidate change once the app plays; push to `main` and CI
does the rest: tests, lint, asset pins, then the signed AAB and APK,
published to the `latest-build` GitHub release (a prerelease that always
moves to the newest build). Pull the AAB from that release. The signing
keystore lives OUTSIDE the repo (owner vault) with its base64 twin in the
KEYSTORE_BASE64 secret; local builds without the keystore stay unsigned.
The owner uploads to Play Console and pastes the notes. Release notes
arrive in chat as bare plain text that fits the 500-character Play field,
counted before handing them over, never assumed. The listing kit and the
console answers live in play-store/play-store-submission-guide.md.

  colliding app, game, or brand on Play, the App Store, or GitHub.
- D-002 (2026-09-04): License is MIT, matching both house apps. Tradeoff
  accepted: anyone may compile a free clone; the moat is the listing, the
  brand, and the craft.
- D-003 (2026-09-04): English only. Revisit only on owner request.
- D-004 (2026-09-04): `applicationId` and namespace are `app.puzzlet`,
  clean, no legacy suffix. A new ID has no scars.
- D-005 (2026-09-04): Toolchain pinned to the house: Gradle 9.5.0, AGP
  9.3.0, Kotlin 2.2.10, Compose BOM 2026.08.00, Java 17, compile/target 37,
  minSdk 24. One house, one discipline.
- D-006 (2026-09-04): M0 dependencies are minimal on purpose: BOM,
  core-ktx, activity-compose, ui, foundation, material3, JUnit. Lifecycle
  and collections-immutable arrive with the M1 host and domain.
- D-007 (2026-09-04): M0 home is brand-only by design (see The game). A
  button that does nothing would be slop wearing a costume.
- D-008 (2026-09-04): Launcher icon is generated, never hand-drawn:
  teal tile, paper puzzle-piece glyph, white monochrome sibling, legacy
  PNGs for API 24-25. `checkIcons` pins them.
- D-009 (2026-09-04): The app owns the whole screen from day one
  (immersive bars, edge-to-edge). A child's toy-box has no chrome.
- D-010 (2026-09-04): `allowBackup` is false and cleartext is off, the
  house posture: there is nothing to back up yet and nothing to fetch ever.
- D-011 (2026-09-04): The toy-box is light-fixed: one warm paper world, no
  night variant, so launch never flashes dark before the first frame. A
  night scheme must earn its place with gameplay (M1).
- D-012 (2026-09-04): Two lifecycle dependencies joined, house-pinned:
  lifecycle-viewmodel-compose and lifecycle-runtime-compose, 2.8.7. The
  host is a ViewModel; collectAsStateWithLifecycle needs its runtime.
- D-013 (2026-09-04): Scenes are pure data (SceneSpec in core) rendered by
  one DrawScope renderer at any size. Scene palettes are content constants
  beside their shapes; UI chrome colors stay in PuzzletColors. Subjects:
  sailboat, rocket, house; all inanimate, no faces, no eyes.
- D-014 (2026-09-04): The play field is a single Canvas; piece outlines are
  bezier paths remembered per piece id and rebuilt only when the cut
  changes. No bitmaps in gameplay; everything stays vector and crisp.
- D-015 (2026-09-04): Touches are forgiving by design: the hit test takes
  the topmost unplaced piece within max(44dp, half piece diagonal) of the
  finger. Snapping is absolute (a piece has exactly one home) with a
  tolerance of 0.38 of the smaller cell side; a placed piece locks.
- D-016 (2026-09-04): The ghost board is always visible at 0.13 alpha; the
  peek coin strengthens it to 0.45. While a piece is held, its slot glows
  honey. A snap answers with a ring burst and one haptic tick. No sounds
  until M2; nothing pitches, nothing sings.
- D-017 (2026-09-04): The ladder is fixed: 4, 6, 9, 12, 16, 20, 24 pieces.
  Board side: min(0.92 x field width, 0.60 (chunky) or 0.78 x field height,
  560dp). Scatter prefers the margins around the board and falls back onto
  the ghost only when a small screen leaves no room; three passes with a
  relaxing minimum distance keep it bounded.
- D-018 (2026-09-04): Resume is in-memory for the session: backing out of a
  started picture keeps it, the shelf dot marks it. Cross-process resume
  arrives in M2 with DataStore.
- D-019 (2026-09-04): Seeded determinism: same picture and difficulty means
  the same cut, the same scatter, every time. Muscle memory and the ghost
  board both depend on it; the seed derives from scene id plus ladder step.
- D-020 (2026-09-04): Sound is the house generator ported: CPython-seeded
  noise, damped non-integer partials, hand-rolled canonical WAVs. Four
  effects: pick (55 ms tap), drop (soft set-down), snap (the woody click
  home), and chime (one struck bell, C5, completion only, 1200 ms guard).
  SoundPool with USAGE_GAME and sonification content, the house config;
  the host infers effects from transitions, the core stays sound-free.
- D-021 (2026-09-04): The sound switch lives on the shelf header, not
  behind a parent gate: there is nothing dangerous to protect (no links,
  no purchases), a child flipping it off harms nobody, and discoverable
  quiet is worth more to parents than a locked switch. Persists in
  DataStore; haptics stay (they are silent, tiny, and part of the click).
- D-022 (2026-09-04): One unfinished picture persists across process death
  (DataStore preferences: scene, ladder step, placed ids). Restore uses the
  same seed, so unplaced pieces land back on their original scatter seats;
  saving happens on back, on restart-clear, and on onStop. Nothing else is
  stored, ever.
- D-023 (2026-09-04): House rule adopted: no composable takes a ViewModel.
  Screens take state and callbacks; the activity wires the host in. This is
  what lets the screenshot harness host every state with no-op callbacks.
- D-024 (2026-09-04): The screenshot harness is the house one: bare
  ComponentActivity, states pushed in, PixelCopy, no compose test rule and
  no Espresso. Captures run in CI at API 35 on the three Play form factors.
- D-025 (2026-09-05): The gallery grows to five scenes: sailboat, rocket,
  house, lighthouse, balloon. All inanimate, no faces, no eyes; translucent
  honey beams and halos are allowed content, and the scene sanity test now
  floors alpha at 25 percent instead of demanding opaque.
- D-026 (2026-09-05): Store art is generated, never hand-drawn:
  :tools:makeArt writes the feature graphic (wordmark + promise beside the
  tilted glyph over a star lattice) and the 512 store icon, lettered in the
  bundled Baloo 2, from the same design constants as the launcher icons.
- D-027 (2026-09-05): The upload keystore lives outside the repo (owner
  vault) with its base64 twin in the KEYSTORE_BASE64 secret and the
  passwords in three more secrets. CI decodes, signs, verifies with
  keytool, and publishes the AAB + APK to the latest-build release. A lost
  keystore is an un-updatable app, so the vault file is sacred.
- D-029 (2026-09-05): The version walk is the owner's law: versionName
  steps 0.1 per release, in lockstep with versionCode (1 equals 0.1, 10
  equals 1.0). The first Google Play appearance is a CLOSED TESTING
  release of versionCode 1, versionName 0.1; its notes live in the
  submission guide. Play's license-testing list keeps paid-app testers
  from being charged.
